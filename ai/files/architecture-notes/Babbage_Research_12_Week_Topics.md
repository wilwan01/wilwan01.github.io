After today’s KV-cache essay, I’d propose these **12 Saturday editions**, alternating AI serving, CPU architecture, memory, and infrastructure economics. Each is a hypothesis to test against measurements—not a predetermined verdict.

| Saturday | Working title | Inversion and quantitative test | Where the policy should move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12 Sep** | **When Separating Prefill and Decode Creates More Work** | Phase isolation versus conversation locality: when repeated state transfer and queueing exceed the interference avoided. Start with multi-turn workloads. [PPD research](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13358v2) | A session scheduler choosing placement per turn, using state location and both latency budgets. |
| **19 Sep** | **Huge Pages, Tiny Hotspots** | Better TLB coverage versus worse data placement: when page-walk savings lose to copying cold bytes and accessing misplaced hot bytes. [TierBPF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12300) | Joint translation and tier-placement policy, informed by subpage heat and useful bytes per migration. |
| **26 Sep** | **Speculative Decoding Can Spend More Than It Saves** | Fewer sequential decoding steps versus extra shared compute: when drafting and verification reduce throughput under concurrency. [ECHO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09603) | Batch-level allocation of speculation, driven by accepted tokens per unit of scarce compute. |
| **3 Oct** | **More Cores, Less Useful Server** | Core-count scaling versus fixed memory and power budgets: identify where additional runnable threads increase queueing without increasing SLO-qualified throughput. | CPU procurement and workload admission based on bandwidth demand and latency, with per-workload stall attribution. |
| **10 Oct** | **Sparsity Saves FLOPs and Buys a Barrier** | MoE arithmetic savings versus expert dispatch, queueing, and synchronization: compare exposed communication time with compute saved at matched quality. [All-to-all scheduling research](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09764v3) | Joint expert placement, replication, routing, and admission; attribute each token’s delay to its critical expert. |
| **17 Oct** | **Pooled Memory Can Pool the Tail** | Reduced stranded capacity versus shared bottlenecks: determine when capacity savings are outweighed by correlated contention and lost goodput. | Pool-wide bandwidth admission and placement, with tenant-attributed queueing and interference measurements. |
| **24 Oct** | **The Fastest Kernel Can Slow the Service** | Local vectorization gains versus frequency, power, and co-runner costs: measure whole-service performance under mixed load on the specific processor. | Compiler multiversioning coordinated with runtime placement and power control; account for performance lost elsewhere. |
| **31 Oct** | **Known-Good Die Does Not Mean Known-Good Product** | Die-yield savings versus assembly loss, D2D overhead, testing, and inventory mismatch: compare cost per delivered system. [CATCH](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15753v1) | Chiplet partitioning jointly optimized with packaging, test strategy, and product-portfolio demand. |
| **7 Nov** | **One CPU SKU Is Not One Latency Distribution** | Nominally interchangeable servers versus fan-out amplification of persistent host variation. Separate silicon and power effects from software noise before quantifying request tails. | Procurement acceptance and scheduler capability classes, grounded in repeatable per-host measurements. |
| **14 Nov** | **A Checkpoint Is Only as Useful as Its Restore Path** | Fast checkpoint creation versus inflexible recovery: when waiting for the original hardware layout costs more than repartitioning state onto available resources. [Universal Checkpointing](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc25-lian.pdf) | Portable state representation coordinated with recovery scheduling; measure time to resumed useful training. |
| **21 Nov** | **The Cheapest Accelerator Can Waste the Most Expensive Megawatt** | Low acquisition cost versus scarce facility power: compare useful throughput and total cost under the actual site power constraint. | One procurement, placement, and power model spanning accelerators, networking, cooling, and deployment timing. |
| **28 Nov** | **The GPU Hour Is the Wrong Unit of Compute** | Hardware rental units versus delivered service: test when hourly-price rankings reverse after model quality, context, latency, and utilization are fixed. | Workload-defined pricing indices and contracts, with reproducible service measurements and explicit basis risk. |

**The most distinctive Babbage pieces are huge pages, within-SKU variation, chiplet product economics, and the megawatt constraint.** They connect architectural mechanisms to decisions a hyperscaler can actually change.

For every edition, I’d require one defensible numerical contrast, one crossover condition, and one named diagnostic that would establish whether the problem exists in production.
