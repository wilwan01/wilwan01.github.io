Patterns in the reviewed Q&A

Fabric mechanisms mattered beyond bandwidth.

Questions probed retries, buffers, programmable controls and mixed-vendor operation. Interoperability did not establish equal performance.

Optics exposed packaging and repair tradeoffs.

Thermal paths, fiber connections and driver placement drew scrutiny. External laser replacement left internal optical-engine repair unresolved.

Metric boundaries changed the interpretation.

Marvell excluded DRAM latency from its expander figure. dMatrix reported a core efficiency metric; NVIDIA clarified the traffic used in its noise test.

Memory expansion needed an access model.

Questions tested host paths, capacity and coherence. Marvell scoped coherence to the expander; IBM discussed added memory-path latency and capacity tradeoffs.

Reliability required system-level recovery.

Stacked-memory mitigations lacked field failure statistics. Huawei’s recovery depended on checkpoints and spare racks, not just a redundant chip.

Implications and concrete vendor asks

Analyst inference: these present-day acceptance tests follow from historical uncertainties. Historical statements and roadmap promises do not establish later delivery.

Scale-up and scale-out
Before design freeze		NVIDIA, AMD, Intel and Broadcom: run matched mixed-vendor tests with incast, background traffic and link faults. Report loaded tail latency, retry limits and recovery. Choose on delivered job performance and operating cost.
KV-cache offload
Before architecture lock		Memory and fabric suppliers: measure the complete GPU-to-memory path, including DRAM, queues, copies and synchronization. Compare local memory, RDMA and coherent options on long-context serving. Offload must lower cost while meeting TTFT and TPOT targets.
Head nodes
Before shortlist		AMD and Intel: compare Venice and DMR with identical accelerator I/O, memory population and host workloads. Include cross-domain shared-data access and contention. Component latency claims cannot substitute for the full serving path.
MI400
Before prototype		AMD: provide workload-matched serving results with explicit precision, quality, memory residency and whole-server energy. Test concurrent traffic and recovery. Advance only if the gain persists beyond accelerator-core measurements.
Optical reliability
Before qualification		NVIDIA, Ayar Labs, Lightmatter and other optics suppliers: disclose internal engine repair, spare requirements and workload interruption. Compare ring and alternative modulation under matched thermal transients and complete power boundaries.
Supporting architectural interpretation

Recurring themes within the reviewed questions and answers. The implications below are architectural interpretation, not a ranking of industry demand.

1. The fabric is judged by behaviour under load

Retries, buffers, programmable controls and mixed-vendor operation recur in the networking review; compatibility did not establish equivalent performance.

Architectural implication: Compare delivered communication and tail latency during contention and failure, not aggregate link bandwidth alone.

2. A component metric can omit the dominant cost

Memory-expander latency excluded DRAM access, core efficiency did not establish whole-card efficiency, and traffic conditions changed noise-test interpretation.

Architectural implication: Define the path, traffic and energy boundary before using a vendor number in a system comparison.

3. Reliability extends to the recovery and repair path

Optical-engine repair remained unresolved beyond replaceable external lasers. Stacked-memory discussion lacked field statistics, while system recovery depended on checkpoints and spare racks.

Architectural implication: Account for the replaceable unit, interruption, spare capacity and recovery procedure—not only device redundancy.

These are present-day evaluation recommendations drawn from the historical evidence, rather than claims that later products were discussed in this conference year.

Fabric performance under stress

Run mixed-vendor tests with incast, background traffic and link faults. Report loaded tail latency, retry limits and recovery; compatibility alone does not establish equivalent performance.

Complete memory paths

Include DRAM, queues, copies and synchronization in GPU-to-memory measurements. Compare local memory, RDMA and coherent paths under the same long-context serving targets.

Repair and recovery

Identify the replaceable unit, spare requirements and workload interruption for optical failures. Qualify thermal transients and full power boundaries, as well as checkpoint and spare-system dependencies.
