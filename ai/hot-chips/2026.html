Implications and concrete vendor asks

Supporting architectural interpretation

Recurring themes within the reviewed questions and answers. The implications below are architectural interpretation, not a ranking of industry demand.

1. The serving workload becomes the common evaluation unit

Performance-methodology, memory-movement and host-balance questions connect accelerator results to precision, context, placement and CPU work.

Architectural implication: Measure matched prefill, decode and MoE with host, NIC and energy costs included; component disclosures alone do not settle cost per token.

2. Capacity and connectivity do not settle ownership

Questions about spill and migration sit alongside unresolved coherence and data-return paths. Protocol names alone did not establish source selection or software obligations.

Architectural implication: Specify who owns KV and shared data, who may cache it, and what migration or cache maintenance costs under concurrency.

3. Operating conditions matter beyond advertised scale

Fabric questions probe routing, shared bandwidth and recovery; SMT questions require workload-specific utilization and isolation comparisons.

Architectural implication: Test useful bandwidth, tail latency and CPU service under competing traffic and failures at the intended scale.

4. Missing answers need evidence reconciliation

Reported live evidence changes some Slack-only answer classifications, but no recording audio was independently reviewed. Open questions are not proof of deliberate avoidance.

Architectural implication: Track unresolved conditions precisely and verify consequential numerical disclosures before relying on them.

These are present-day evaluation recommendations drawn from the historical evidence, rather than claims that later products were discussed in this conference year.

CPU head-node selection

AMD and Intel: explain cache ownership and peer-cache versus memory sourcing. Compare producer–consumer latency, useful bandwidth and CPU overhead during concurrent GPU and NIC traffic. Provide diagnostic counters.

MI400 evaluation

AMD: run matched prefill, decode and MoE workloads with the same model quality, precision, context, batch and software. Report throughput, tail latency, energy per token, memory and link traffic, host utilization, system price and availability.

Fabric procurement

Document coherence, ordering and interoperability guarantees. Test all-to-all and small-message collectives at the intended scale under competing traffic, congestion and failure.

KV placement

Explain ownership, cache maintenance and migration. Compare offload with HBM residency at the same quality and service target, including bytes moved, writes, tail latency, recovery, endurance and cost per usable capacity.
