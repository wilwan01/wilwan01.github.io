Patterns in the reviewed Q&A

Memory placement and coherence drew sustained probing.

AMD confirmed directed probes but left protocol details unresolved. Questions also tested HBM access efficiency, PIM interfaces and memory overhead.

Runtime flexibility needed practical detail.

Questions examined partitioning, debugging, context switching and concurrent models. Some configuration features were explained while their overhead remained unquantified.

Sparse gains needed full cost accounting.

Onyx questions exposed conversion and metadata costs. Meta separated evaluated or analytical benefits from deployed improvements.

Cooling was examined at system level.

Questions covered rack limits, maintenance, capex and prototype evidence. Chip heat removal alone did not settle the facility decision.

Optical integration still needed qualification.

Intel called OCI a technology demonstration. Laser history from pluggable modules did not establish reliability in the proposed CPU package.

Implications and concrete vendor asks

Analyst inference: these present-day acceptance tests follow from historical uncertainties. Historical statements and roadmap promises do not establish later delivery.

Head nodes
Before shortlist		AMD and Intel: expose Venice and DMR cross-die access paths, coherence behaviour and loaded latency. Test CPU work alongside accelerator traffic. Prefer the topology with better delivered host service and memory access under contention.

Before prototype		AMD: specify supported partitions, runtime reconfiguration, isolation and context-switch cost. Demonstrate concurrent models and compare sparse/quantized gains after conversion, metadata and quality costs. Require results from the intended software release.
KV-cache offload
Before architecture lock		Memory and accelerator suppliers: show dynamic KV allocation, eviction, synchronization and placement across memory tiers. Test long contexts and shared prefixes. Change the design only when capacity savings survive transfer and scheduling overhead.
Scale-up and scale-out
Before design freeze		Optics and network suppliers: provide loaded latency, error recovery, thermal-transient qualification and field-replaceable boundaries. Separate technology demonstrations from qualified product results before choosing package-integrated optics.
Cooling
Before facility commitment		Server and cooling suppliers: price the full retrofit, residual air cooling and maintenance burden. Measure sustained workload energy and rack flow limits. Accept a design on facility economics and availability, not a chip-cooling result alone.
Supporting architectural interpretation

Recurring themes within the reviewed questions and answers. The implications below are architectural interpretation, not a ranking of industry demand.

1. Resource flexibility exposes control costs

Memory placement, directed probes, partitioning, context switching and concurrent models drew questions across the reviewed sessions.

Architectural implication: A configurable resource is useful only when ownership, isolation and switching overhead fit the workload.

2. Efficiency gains need their overheads restored

Sparse-compute questions exposed conversion and metadata costs. Cooling questions expanded the boundary to racks, facilities and maintenance.

Architectural implication: Test the net gain after representation changes and infrastructure costs, rather than carrying a component ratio into a system business case.

3. Demonstration and deployment remain different milestones

Optical I/O was described as a technology demonstration; historical laser reliability did not establish reliability in the proposed package. Cooling also required prototype and operational evidence.

Architectural implication: Qualify the proposed configuration itself, including thermal conditions, repair and software release.

These are present-day evaluation recommendations drawn from the historical evidence, rather than claims that later products were discussed in this conference year.

Memory placement and ownership

Request cross-die paths, coherence behaviour and loaded latency. Test dynamic allocation, eviction and synchronization across memory tiers, including shared prefixes.

Runtime and useful acceleration

Measure partitioning, reconfiguration, isolation and context-switch cost. Compare sparse and quantized gains after conversion, metadata and quality costs.

Facility and optical qualification

Distinguish a technology demonstration from qualified product evidence. Price retrofit work, residual air cooling and maintenance; measure sustained energy and rack flow limits.
